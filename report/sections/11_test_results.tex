\section{Final evaluation on the untouched test set}
\label{sec:test}

The three tuned models were evaluated once on the 9{,}758 people of the test
set, each with the threshold frozen during training
(Section~\ref{sec:tuning}). Nothing was changed after this evaluation.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Test-set results of the three tuned models (thresholds frozen on
the development set). Always predicting \negc{} would reach 76.1\% accuracy.}
\label{tab:test}
\small
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrrrr}
\toprule
Model & ROC-AUC & AP & Log-loss & Accuracy & Precision & Recall & F1 & Thr. \\
\midrule
Logistic regression & 0.910 & 0.773 & 0.317 & 0.845 & 0.658 & 0.732 & 0.693 & 0.37 \\
Random forest       & 0.918 & 0.805 & 0.353 & 0.847 & 0.651 & \textbf{0.778} & 0.709 & 0.62 \\
LightGBM            & \textbf{0.926} & \textbf{0.821} & \textbf{0.297} & \textbf{0.863} & \textbf{0.702} & 0.744 & \textbf{0.722} & 0.54 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Test against cross-validation.} The test scores are slightly below
the out-of-fold scores of Table~\ref{tab:models-tuned}: about $-0.01$ AP and
$-0.01$ F1 for every model (LightGBM: AP 0.832 to 0.821, F1 0.733 to 0.722).
This small drop is the one expected from selecting hyperparameters and
thresholds on the same folds (Section~\ref{sec:tuning}); it is far too small to
suggest we overfitted the folds. The ranking is unchanged: LightGBM is first
on every metric except recall, then the random forest, then the logistic
regression. Compared with the 76.1\% of the trivial model, LightGBM reaches
86.3\% accuracy, and above all it finds 74\% of the \pos{} people with a
precision of 70\%, which the trivial model cannot do at all.
