\section{Alternatives tested, limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{An alternative we tested: a ``missing'' category}

Are the missing job values random (Section~\ref{sec:eda})? On the development
set, the 2{,}233 people with a missing \code{workclass} all have a missing
\code{occupation} too, and only 9.4\% of the people with a missing occupation
earn \pos, against 23.9\% overall: they are mostly people without a job, whom
the most-frequent imputation gives the most common job. A separate ``missing''
category keeps this information, but on the untuned models it gains only 0.003
AP for the logistic regression and 0.001 for LightGBM, with the same F1
(Table~\ref{tab:missing}, appendix): of the order of the fold-to-fold
variation, so we kept the most-frequent imputation rather than re-run the
tuning; a separate category remains more principled in production.

\subsection{Overfitting: what we checked and what we did not}
\label{sec:overfitting}

\textbf{Overfitting the validation folds: checked.} The 95 trials and the
thresholds were chosen on the same 5 folds, so the best CV scores are
optimistic by construction. The test set measures by how much: every model
loses about 0.01 AP (LightGBM 0.832 to 0.821) and the ranking does not change.
\textbf{Memorizing the training rows: checked for the logistic regression
only}, whose train--validation gap stays below 0.004
(Section~\ref{sec:regularization}). For the tree models we never computed a
training score; the evidence is indirect (limiting the forest's depth and leaf
size raised its CV AP by 0.064, and the test scores hold). A table of train,
validation and test AP, and a LightGBM learning curve, would measure the gap.

\subsection{Limitations}

\textbf{Old data:} the census dates from 1994, so the model would need
retraining on recent data. \textbf{Possible leakage:} \code{capital-gain} and
\code{capital-loss} are declared for the same tax year as the income.
\textbf{F1 stands in for business costs:} with real costs per error, the
threshold should minimize the expected cost. \textbf{Threshold chosen on fold
models} trained on 80\% of the development set, then applied to the refitted
model. \textbf{Probabilities not calibrated} as-is
(Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). \textbf{No fairness audit:} the model uses
\code{sex} and \code{race}, and the share of \pos{} is 30\% for men against
11\% for women; the audit was out of scope, but would be needed before any
real use.

\subsection{Conclusion}

Our final model, a tuned LightGBM in one Pipeline, reaches an AP of 0.821 and
an F1 of 0.722 on the untouched test set, within 0.01 of cross-validation.
What mattered most was the model (+0.05 AP over the logistic regression), then
the decision threshold, then the engineered features. Its decisions are reliable; its probabilities, inflated by the
class weight, need recalibrating (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}).
