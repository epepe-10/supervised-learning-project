\section{Modelling progression}
\label{sec:models}

We compared three models of increasing flexibility, inside the same Pipeline,
on the same folds and with the same metrics. Their equations (sigmoid and
penalized log-loss, Gini and entropy splits, gradient boosting update) are in
Appendix~\ref{sec:model-details}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Logistic regression}: linear in the log-odds, interpretable and
  probabilistic, which makes it a strong baseline. The \code{saga} solver
  supports the three penalties: \code{l1\_ratio} sets the mix (0 = Ridge, 1 =
  Lasso, in between = Elastic Net) and $C = 1/\lambda$ its strength.
  \item \textbf{Random forest}: many deep trees, each trained on a bootstrap
  sample with a random subset of features at each split, then averaged.
  Averaging decorrelated trees mainly reduces the variance.
  \item \textbf{LightGBM}: gradient boosting. Small trees are added one at a
  time, each fitted to the errors of the current ensemble, which mainly reduces
  the bias. We chose LightGBM over XGBoost or CatBoost, which implement the same
  algorithm, because it is the fastest, which matters when each tuning trial
  means five fits; CatBoost's strength, high-cardinality categories, does not
  apply here (\code{native-country}, the largest, has about forty).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What each step bought.} Feature engineering added 0.021 AP to the
logistic regression (Section~\ref{sec:features}). The untuned random forest is
\emph{worse} than the logistic regression (AP 0.750 against 0.784, log-loss
0.446): with the default settings, its trees grow until each leaf is pure, so
they memorize the training folds and the averaged probabilities are poorly
calibrated. LightGBM is the best model on every metric: AP 0.831 (+0.047 over
the logistic regression), ROC-AUC 0.929, log-loss 0.275 and F1 0.731. The gaps
between models (at least 0.03 AP) are more than five times the variation of AP
across folds (at most 0.006), so the ranking is not an artefact of the split.

\paragraph{What tuning bought.} Tuning mattered most where the defaults were
worst. The random forest gains 0.064 AP (0.750 to 0.814) and 0.038 F1 once its
depth and leaf size are limited: it stops memorizing the training folds and
moves from last to second place. This is the bias--variance trade-off: a deep
tree with tiny leaves has low bias but high variance, and the tuned depth of 14
with at least 3 rows per leaf is the compromise chosen by cross-validation. The
logistic regression does not move (AP 0.784, F1 0.699): with three
hyperparameters and a linear boundary, its defaults were already close to its
best (Section~\ref{sec:regularization}). LightGBM gains almost nothing (AP
0.831 to 0.832), because its defaults were already well suited to this table.
The ranking is the same on every metric except recall, where the balanced
random forest is ahead. The final model is the one with the best out-of-fold AP
after tuning, LightGBM, chosen before the test set is used.
