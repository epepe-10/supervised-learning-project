\section{Modelling progression}
\label{sec:models}

We compared three models of increasing flexibility, inside the same Pipeline,
on the same folds and with the same metrics.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Logistic regression}: linear in the log-odds, interpretable and
  probabilistic, which makes it a strong baseline. The \code{saga} solver
  supports the three penalties: \code{l1\_ratio} sets the mix (0 = Ridge, 1 =
  Lasso, in between = Elastic Net) and $C = 1/\lambda$ its strength.
  \item \textbf{Random forest}: many deep trees, each trained on a bootstrap
  sample with a random subset of features at each split, then averaged.
  Averaging decorrelated trees reduces the variance, and trees capture
  non-linear effects and interactions without feature engineering.
  \item \textbf{LightGBM}: gradient boosting. Trees are added one at a time,
  each one correcting the errors of the current ensemble, and grown leaf-wise.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What each step bought.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Feature engineering} added 0.021 AP to the logistic regression
  (Section~\ref{sec:features}).
  \item \textbf{The untuned random forest is worse than the logistic
  regression} (AP 0.750 against 0.784, log-loss 0.446). With the default
  settings, the trees grow until each leaf is pure: they memorize the training
  folds, and the averaged probabilities are poorly calibrated. The forest needs
  its complexity to be tuned (depth, leaf size), which Section~\ref{sec:tuning}
  does.
  \item \textbf{LightGBM is the best model on every metric}: AP 0.831 (+0.047
  over the logistic regression), ROC-AUC 0.929, log-loss 0.275 and F1 0.731.
  The gaps between models (at least 0.03 AP) are about ten times larger than
  the variation of AP across folds (at most 0.006), so the ranking is not an
  artefact of the split.
\end{itemize}
